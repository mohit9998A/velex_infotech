Free AI chatbots are genuinely good now, which makes the buying decision harder rather than easier. The question is no longer whether the free tier works — it usually does — but whether the specific thing your business needs sits on the wrong side of a line that free tiers all draw in roughly the same place.

Here is where that line falls, and how to tell which side you are on.

## Is a free AI chatbot good enough for a business?

A free AI chatbot is good enough when it only needs to answer questions from information you can paste into it. It stops being enough the moment it needs to read your live data or write anything back into your systems.

That is the line, and it is remarkably consistent across providers. Free tiers give you a conversational interface over static content — your FAQ, your policies, a scraped version of your website. What they withhold is the integration layer: reading a live order status, checking real inventory, writing a booking into your calendar, updating a CRM record.

The reason is commercial rather than technical. Integration is where the value is, so integration is what gets charged for. Any evaluation that only tests conversation quality will conclude the free tier is fine, because conversation quality is the part that has been commoditised.

## What do free tiers actually limit?

Message volume, integrations, branding removal, human handover and data retention — in roughly that order of how quickly you hit them.

- **Message or conversation caps.** Usually generous enough for a low-traffic site and restrictive the moment a campaign works.
- **Integrations.** The decisive one. Reading and writing to your own systems is almost universally a paid feature.
- **Branding.** Free tiers carry the vendor's name, which for a premium-positioned business is a real cost even if it is not a line item.
- **Human handover.** Routing a conversation to a person with context attached is frequently paid, and it is exactly what you need when the bot fails.
- **Data handling.** Free tiers often reserve broader rights over conversation data, which matters if customers type anything sensitive.

The last one deserves more attention than it gets. If your chatbot handles customer questions, customers will eventually paste order numbers, email addresses and occasionally things they should not. Where that goes, and how long it is kept, is worth reading before deploying rather than after.

## When does a free chatbot actively cost you money?

When it answers confidently and wrongly, or when it cannot hand off. Both convert an interested customer into an annoyed one, and neither shows up in the tool's own analytics.

A chatbot working from static content cannot know that a policy changed last week, that an item is out of stock, or that this particular customer has an open complaint. It will answer anyway, in a confident tone, because that is what the underlying model does. The customer takes that answer as your position.

The handover failure is more common and more damaging. A customer who cannot reach a person after two failed attempts does not usually try a third channel — they leave. If your business runs on inbound enquiries, the cost of that is measurable from your own numbers and is frequently larger than the subscription you avoided.

## Free tools versus a custom build — how should you decide?

Decide on integration depth and volume. If the bot only needs to answer from documents and traffic is modest, use a free or low-cost tool. If it must transact inside your systems, or handles enough volume that per-conversation pricing bites, a build becomes the cheaper option.

There is a middle path most businesses should consider first: a paid tier of an off-the-shelf tool. It is unglamorous advice and usually correct. Paid tiers unlock integrations and handover, cost far less than a build, and are appropriate for anything that follows a standard pattern — bookings, support FAQs, lead capture.

A custom build earns its cost in three situations: when the workflow is genuinely specific to your business, when per-conversation pricing at your volume exceeds a build's amortised cost, or when data handling requirements rule out sending conversations through a third party. Outside those, the honest recommendation is to buy.

Velex Infotech builds custom WhatsApp and voice agents, and we tell prospects to start with an off-the-shelf tool more often than not — because a business that has not yet measured its conversation volume cannot justify a build, and finding out is cheap.

## What about WhatsApp specifically?

WhatsApp is different because Meta charges regardless of your tooling. There is no free path to an automated WhatsApp presence — the WhatsApp Business app has no API, and the Business API carries Meta's own per-conversation fees.

This catches people out. A vendor quoting a low monthly fee for "WhatsApp chatbot" may be quoting only their software, with Meta's conversation charges arriving separately and scaling with your volume. Service conversations inside the 24-hour window a customer opens are the cheap part; template messages you initiate outside it are billed per conversation by category and country.

The practical consequence is that WhatsApp economics should be modelled on your actual message mix before committing, not estimated from a monthly software price. It is also why WhatsApp bots reward being genuinely useful — a bot that resolves the query inside the free window costs meaningfully less than one that triggers follow-ups.

## How should you evaluate a chatbot before deploying it?

Test it with your ten hardest real customer questions, not the examples in the vendor's demo. Then test what happens when it does not know, and how a customer reaches a person.

Pull the questions from your actual inbox or call log. Include the ambiguous ones, the ones where the customer is annoyed, and at least one where the correct answer is "that depends." A tool that handles clean questions and collapses on messy ones will collapse in production, because production is messy.

Then check three failure behaviours: what it does with a question outside its knowledge, whether it invents an answer under pressure, and how many steps a customer needs to reach a human. Those three predict deployment outcomes better than any accuracy figure a vendor publishes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are free AI chatbots safe for customer data?**
Read the retention and training terms before deploying. Free tiers frequently reserve broader rights over conversation content, and customers will paste more sensitive information than you expect.

**Can a free chatbot integrate with my CRM?**
Rarely. Integration is the main thing free tiers withhold, because it is where the commercial value sits.

**Will customers know it is a bot?**
Yes, and you should tell them anyway. Disclosure costs less than discovery, and transparency obligations for automated systems are increasing in several jurisdictions.

**Is a free WhatsApp chatbot possible?**
Not for automation. The WhatsApp Business app has no API, and the Business API carries Meta's per-conversation charges regardless of which vendor you use.

**When is a custom chatbot worth building?**
When it must transact inside your systems, when per-conversation costs at your volume exceed a build, or when data handling rules out a third party. Otherwise buy a paid tier.
